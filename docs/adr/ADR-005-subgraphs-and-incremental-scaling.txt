ADR-005: Scale Through Relevant Subgraphs and Incremental Updates

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-09-29

Context

Rendering and laying out every known device and connection cannot scale without
bound. Edges, labels, rich styling, animations, and layouts may dominate browser
cost. A faster state library or experimental renderer alone does not solve an
unbounded visual workload.

Decision

Scale primarily by rendering the relevant subgraph: filter/load by site or
rack, bound traversal depth, expand neighbors on demand, and cluster/collapse
groups. Within the visible graph, use normalized indexes, one long-lived
Cytoscape instance, incremental batched changes, inexpensive styles, and
layouts only for meaningful topology changes. Add workers or experimental
rendering only after representative profiling.

Consequences

- Product UX must support scope, expansion, and aggregation as data grows.
- Backend APIs will eventually need subgraph/search/pagination capabilities.
- Users may not see the entire data center simultaneously, but retain a more
  responsive and understandable view.
- Performance support claims require benchmark evidence and edge-density data.

Alternatives considered

- Always render the full graph: simple mental model but creates an unbounded
  browser workload and can become visually unusable.
- Rely on experimental WebGL alone: may help rendering but does not eliminate
  layout, memory, data-transfer, or comprehension costs.
- Premature worker architecture: increases serialization and coordination
  complexity before a measured bottleneck exists.
